“I do not know,” F.R.I.E.N.D. said, “but there is a surplus of multiversal professions in this universe.”
“Where do I find one of these professionals?”
“Kathmandu, Nepal.”
“Nepal? How do we get there?”
“You can use the jetpack supplied by Cisco Ramon. However, it is an approximately eight-thousand-mile journey. Even at your top speed, the journey would take a few hours.”
“Okay. I hope you got a food fabricator in there, my F.R.I.E.N.D. This is gonna take a while.” With that, Richard was off. He flew through the skies westward at Mach Five, the suit protecting him from the force that would otherwise tear his body apart.
“Woooooooo!” Richard exclaimed in exhilaration as he soared. “This is amazing! Why don’t we have these in my universe?”
“It is most likely due to your universe lacking the scientific development required to construct such a technological marvel.”
“You didn’t have to be so blunt about it.”
“Have you guys even discovered dwarf star alloy? Or Pizzazium Infinionite?”
“You’re not being much of a F.R.I.E.N.D. right now. We’re gonna be spending a lot of time together now. You need to lighten up.”
“I was programmed with one imperative: to protect you. Nothing more, nothing less. Emotion isn’t part of my purpose.”
“Don’t worry. I’ll get through that hardware in no time.”

Hours went by, and Richard finally arrived in Kathmandu.
“F.R.I.E.N.D., we’re in Kathmandu. Where now?” Richard asked.
“We need to find a sacred temple known as Kamar-Taj,” F.R.I.E.N.D. responded. “Follow the map directions I am currently displaying on your Heads-Up Display.” A holographic trail appeared on the road, guiding Richard to where he needed to go. Richard walked along the trail while taking in the sights: merchants selling produce in the markets, civilians conversing with beggars, children playing in the street as their parents lovingly watched.
“This city is so peaceful. Everyone is so connected. You can feel the raw love.” Richard’s mood lowered as he slowed his walking pace and went silent.
“I sense an imbalance of serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine in your brain,” F.R.I.E.N.D. commented. “Is there something wrong?”
“No, it’s just…” Richard started. “This is the third universe hop, and I’m still not home yet. What if I never get back?”
“You were bullied constantly every day. Your boss is hunting you down, trying to kill you. What could you miss about your universe?” Richard instantly thought of an answer. At the moment, he had only one person on his mind.
“Something you’ll never understand about being human.” Richard kept going, trying to walk off the sombre mood he felt within. The past few days had been a rollercoaster for him, but this was the first time in a while that he had been fully emotionally invested in his situation. Up until that point, Richard had one continuous state: ‘indifferent optimism,’ but now he had a newfound emotional depth he wasn’t ready for. The melancholy was quickly replaced with vigilance when he sensed the ground rumble.

The streets were stormed and raided by creatures stripped right out of a child’s nightmares. These monstrosities of nature had freakish, pale skin: boiled, torn, exposing flesh. Their eyes popped out of their heads, glowing a bloody red. They had wings ripping out of their spines and large fangs.
“F.R.I.E.N.D., what are those things?” Richard asked, concerned and alert.
“They are demons, Richard,” F.R.I.E.N.D. stated sharply. “Your body is rapidly producing thalamus, glutamate, and hypothalamus. What do you need me to do?”
“That’s called thrill, my F.R.I.E.N.D., … and fear. Get ready to fight.” Richard charged into battle. The gauntlets of his armour gathered nanoparticles to form a sword and shield. He rushed the demons, impaling some and slashing their necks. One lunged at him, and he blocked it with his shield before kicking it away. Richard took out about a hundred of them, but hundreds more replaced them. Overwhelmed with enemies, Richard was knocked to the ground and piled on top of.
“You are just lying on the ground,” F.R.I.E.N.D. commented.
“Well, if you haven’t noticed,” Richard sarcastically stated, “I’m kinda being dog-piled right now.”
“Do something then. The suit is being torn apart.”
“Do what?! There’s nothing I can do!”
“You know what? I’m taking over.” F.R.I.E.N.D. stimulated a particular section of Richard’s brain, forcing him to unleash his powers in one large pulse of energy. Richard screamed in agony as he was pushed to his limits, launching all the demons back, killing them instantly.
“What was that?!”
“I saved you, Richard.”
“First of all, since when could I do that?”
“There’s a lot about your powers you don’t know. For example…” F.R.I.E.N.D. continued her puppeteering. In the blink of an eye and a flash of red energy, Richard was suddenly on a rooftop.
“Did I just teleport?!”
“Your powers have grown exponentially as of late. You can now harness pure energy into constructs, including rifts in spacetime. In short, yes, you can teleport.”
“Cool, but that’s not the point! You can’t just hijack my body, and you certainly can’t perform abilities I don’t understand!”
“I understand them, and you need to give your all to fight threats like this.”
“No, I don’t! I need to give what I can give, and you ‘giving my all’ almost killed me! You shouldn’t be tasked with saving lives because YOU DON’T KNOW THE FIRST THING ABOUT BEING ALIVE! I’M SHUTTING YOU OFF!”
“I was trying to—” F.R.I.E.N.D. was cut off by Richard pressing the button on the side of his helmet, powering the AI down.

He sweated profusely, his muscles spasmed, his nerves screamed in anguish. As his heart rate declined and breathing became difficult, it didn’t take long for Richard to lose grip on his consciousness. As his vision began to blur, something came into view. A woman stood over him. Though the sight lacked clarity, he noticed the distinct blue garments she wore. His visor light turned off, his armour shut down, and his vision went black.

The other, Wong, had short, closely cropped black hair and a trimmed moustache and goatee, wearing layered robes in deep purples and reds, decorated with embroidered patterns and wrapped in braided leather-like arm pieces.
“What’s wrong with him?” Stephen asked frantically.
“He was hit with a boulder's worth of venom from that thing!” America said in a panic. “It’s leaking into his bloodstream, and it’s acting quickly. He’ll be dead soon, an hour if we’re lucky.”
“You’re a surgeon, Stephen!” Wong stated. “You can save him!”
“I can synthesise an antivenom, but the armour he’s in is locked,” Stephen said. “We need to find a way to get him out before we can take any further action.”

“What are you gonna do?” Stephen asked.
“Our city is under attack,” Wong said. “We must prepare for war.”

Richard awoke. He stood up from the table he was lying on and looked around. He saw some kind of sacred temple, mystic writings on the walls.
“Where am I?” Richard called out to whoever might be listening.
“Welcome, Richard Cox, to Kamar-Taj,” someone answered.
“Who was that?” The figure stepped into the light. She had a bare crown and a fair complexion. Her features were striking and austere. She was seen in a bright, golden-yellow hooded robe with a black sash. Her image was tinted blue with transparency.
“I am the Ancient One,” she said, “Or was, anyway.”
“Was?” Richard asked.
“I died seven years ago, killed by a man named Kaecilius. This is an astral projection of my consciousness.”
“So, what happened to me?”
“Your bloodstream was contaminated with atraxotoxin: venom from a Sydney funnel-web spider.”
“That’s what that thing was?”
“Yes, a very dangerous creature. Even more dangerous, enhanced and enlarged with chaos magic. You fell unconscious and were on the brink of death and—”
“And you saved me?”
“Not quite. We used magic to slow the flow of the neurotoxin in your system while we tried to open your suit of armour so we could apply the antivenom. You’re still very close to death. My former protégé, Stephen Strange, used magic to force your soul out of your physical form. You’re an astral projection as well.” Richard looked at his hands and saw the same blue-tinted transparency that was on her. It was true.
“People can still hear and see us like this, right?”
“Yes, just no physical contact.”
“Of course…” Richard hovered towards his lifeless husk on the table. “Eject!” At his command, the armour opened up, allowing access to his body.
“Brilliant!” the Ancient One exclaimed. “But the antivenom could take hours to be formed, extracted and purified, even with magical acceleration.”
“Better than six months.” The Ancient One was surprised by his knowledge of medical development. Richard noticed her shock and joked, “What? I know some things.”
“You really expect me to believe that you found an organisation of sorcerers that protects the world from cosmic threats?” Ellery remarked sceptically.
“So, you can believe me about the multiverse, superpowers and time travel, but you draw the line at magic?” Richard asked.
“… Touché…”

Richard took advantage of his new astral existence. He floated through walls, exploring the complex that was Kamar-Taj. In there, he found a man working with magic and chemicals in a lab.
“Hello,” his voice echoed through the room.
“Hello there, ghost,” the man sarcastically remarked. “How’s the land of the living?”
“My name is Richard Cox, the Weaver.”
“Richard Cox? Your parents must’ve really hated you.”
“I get that a lot. Is that the atraxotoxin antivenom?”
“Oh, so you’re the helpless soul I’m trying to save?”
“Yeah.”
“Well, I’m Doctor Stephen Strange, Master of the New York Sanctum and the former Sorcerer Supreme.”
“Strange?”
“Better than Cox.”

“This is the Cloak of Levitation, a sentient cloak that, as the name entails, allows me to levitate.”
“Cool… And the amulet?”
“This is the Eye of Agamotto. It used to hold the Time Stone, one of the six Infinity Stones, and allowed me to control time.”
“What happened to the Time Stone?”
“An evil alien named Thanos stole it and used it, along with the other Stones, to fulfil his self-righteous goal of wiping out half of all life in the universe, including myself, before shredding the stones down to atoms.”
“You couldn’t stop him?”
“We tried. Some of the most powerful heroes in the universe, we tried.”
“I was reading about the universe on the way here. Apparently, you fought this Thanos guy on a planet called Titan, and you tried to remove the gauntlet he had the Stones in?”
“Yeah.”
“You had the combined forces of ‘Mantis,’ ‘Drax,’ ‘Spider-Man,’ ‘Iron Man,’ ‘Peter Quill,’ and yourself holding him down. How could you not take him?”
“Peter Quill jeopardised the plan in rage when he found out Thanos killed his girlfriend, Gamora, in pursuit of the Soul Stone and Tony Stark, Iron Man, had to stop taking off the gauntlet to hold Peter back.”
“‘Nebula’ told him, right? Assuming that his impulsive behaviour was normal for him,” Strange nodded in agreement to this statement. “Why did she think telling him that information would end well?” The comment baffled Strange. “More importantly, she wasn’t helping to take the glove off, so couldn’t she have held back Quill rather than Tony having to let go of the gauntlet to do so?”
“Well, since you seem to be so smart, why don’t you come help me with this?”
“I would, but I’m a bit preoccupied with, you know, dying.”
“Right… Anyway, Wong wants to see you outside.” Richard floated out of the room.

Leading them was the Sorcerer Supreme and former librarian, Wong. Wong noticed Richard’s presence and excused himself from his students to tend to the patient.

I was so scared by it that I triggered my powers by myself, and the portal swallowed me and my parents. I never saw them again.”

Strange steadily reached down as the needle pierced Richard’s skin, penetrating his vein beneath as the antivenom entered the bloodstream. Richard’s heart rate incrementally increased, stabilising.
“He’s escaping bradycardia,” Strange commented with hopeful relief. Richard reached a regular eighty-five beats per minute, but there was something… off. His heart rate didn’t stop soaring. “His pulse is way off the charts.” Strange continued to examine Richard’s physical condition: bulges of tissue on his face and neck, hives across his forearm, breath barely distinguishable. He was having an allergic reaction to the antivenom. He was entering tachycardia. America looked up at the heart monitor.
“Stephen…” she said, “A hundred and fifty-two is… bad, right?”

The thick sludge that covered the streets was overlaid with a more dilute red. Not even the civilians were safe. No man, woman, or child was safe from the rage. If they had the mercy of surviving the satanic gremlins, they weren’t given such privileges by the Sydney funnel-web spider. An ordinary pedestrian wasn’t blessed with a mystical safeguard from the spider’s atraxotoxin and was instead graced with mortality. Merciless razors slashed throats, dagger-like appendages pierced hearts, sharp fangs ripped bodies. No one was safe.
